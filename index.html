Em dash cleanup (all 16 pages)

Why: the site's copy was full of em dashes (the long "—" character) used in place of commas, colons, and periods. A dash here and there is normal writing, but this density is a well-known tell of AI-generated copy, and in 2026 a lot of visitors (and potential clients) recognize it instantly. It reads as "nobody proofread this," which undercuts the professional image the rest of the site works hard for. Cleaning it up makes the copy read like it was written by a person.

What was done:

  • Rewrote every em dash in body copy with real punctuation, chosen case by case: colons before lists, periods where a new sentence starts, commas for natural continuations. About 280 spots across the 16 pages.
  • Number ranges like "$28 - $42" and "Mon - Fri 8am - 5pm" now use a plain hyphen, the standard way to write ranges.
  • Page titles and the structured data Google reads were cleaned the same way.
  • Also caught the hidden ones: dashes written as HTML codes and dashes inside the invisible search-engine data blocks.
  • Kept exactly one, on purpose: the "Best Trade Jobs — Skilled Trade Careers" label in the careers page hero, where the dash is a visual design separator between two labels, not punctuation.
  • Verified: across all 16 rendered pages, that one label is the only dash remaining, and all the structured data still parses correctly.
